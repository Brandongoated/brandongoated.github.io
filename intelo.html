AI internship, summer 2026

Sales contact database for Intelo.ai

I turned three overlapping contact lists into one ranked database the sales team could work straight from, and I did it with AI instead of code.

Company
Intelo.ai
When
July to August 2026
My role
AI intern, sales side
Tools
Claude Cowork, Apollo.io, HubSpot

The problem

Intelo.ai is a startup that builds AI for retail merchandising. The sales team was reaching out to European retail and luxury companies, but the contacts lived in several places, overlapped with each other, and had no order to them. Nobody could look at the list and say who to call first.

What I did

  1. Built the ideal customer profile (ICP). An ICP is a description of the kind of company, and the kind of job title inside it, that is actually worth going after. I worked out both.
  2. Scored and tiered around 400 companies. I ran every company against the ICP and sorted them into tiers, from the best fit down to the ones that were only adjacent.
  3. Pulled and merged the contacts. I pulled contacts in Apollo.io and merged them with what the team already had in HubSpot and from an industry event list.
  4. Cleaned out the duplicates. Merging three sources creates a lot of repeats, so I flagged and removed them.
  5. Ranked everyone by priority. The end product was a worklist, not a spreadsheet dump. Each contact had a priority and a status so the team knew who to hit first.

How I did it

The whole project ran inside Claude Cowork, an AI tool that works across your files and apps. I did not write a single line of code. The skill was in telling the AI exactly what a good prospect looks like, checking its work, and catching where it got things wrong.

The result

The sales team ran their outreach off the database I built. I got to see my work put to use before I left, which was the best part of the summer.

What I took from it

  • How outreach to customers actually works, from picking the target to the first contact
  • How to use AI to do real business work, not just answer questions
  • A crash course in how a startup runs day to day
  • How to think about a problem the way a business does
